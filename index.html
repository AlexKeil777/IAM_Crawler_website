The idea is simple

Read the market.
Understand what’s on the page.

A conventional crawler depends on knowing where to look and how each shop is built. Change the page, and the extraction rules may need to change with it.

The IAM Crawler reads offers in context. AI handles differences in layout, language, and product descriptions, so the approach can follow relevant shops as the market evolves.

Aftermarket meets intelligence.
Different formats.
The same understanding.

Product grids, long descriptions, marketplace listings. Luna interprets the page’s content rather than relying on a different extraction template for every shop.

Versatility, built into the reading.

A matching number
is only the beginning.

A pair isn’t a single disc. A repair kit isn’t one of its components. The crawler checks fit, variant, quantity and condition, then uses further reads and cross-shop evidence when a judgement needs support.

Questionable offers are excluded, with a reason.

A customer’s journey.
A considered stopping point.

Discover relevant shops, search for a part, read the offers. The crawler follows the same broad journey as a shopper and stops at defined coverage targets, rather than chasing endless results.

A focused view of the offers a buyer encounters.

The shops can change.
The approach still works.

Shop discovery is refreshed periodically. New entrants can be considered, unavailable shops can be left behind, and page changes don’t require a new shop-specific parser.

Less maintenance. Less dependence on a frozen shop list.

A note on coverage +

The crawler produces a focused market sample, not an exhaustive census. Shop access, geography and AI uncertainty can limit coverage. Discovery refreshes periodically; the default 40-offer and five-shop targets depend on availability. Brand quality ratings are AI assessments with recorded reasons and sources.
